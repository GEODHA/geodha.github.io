#### ❓ What's the point of submitting reports?

Every report helps build a real-time map of what's happening in our communities. When we document blackspots, clean areas, or recurring issues, it creates evidence that can be used to raise awareness, highlight patterns, and guide action. Over time, this crowdsourced data becomes a valuable resource for the public, civic bodies, and media to push for better infrastructure and cleaner cities.

#### 📲 Why should I use this app?

GEODHA makes it easy for anyone to take part in civic action — no need for a campaign or a big group. By simply sharing what you see around you, you're helping build a collective picture of your city's waste and infrastructure challenges. Even small reports can spark big changes when they're visible, verified, and backed by the community.

#### 🧠 Who runs GEODHA? Is this a business?

GEODHA is currently being developed by a small independent team during a demo phase. We don't make money from it, and there are no ads or monetized features. Our goal is to show how open civic data can support better decisions — by governments, communities, and changemakers alike.

#### 🤝 Are you like cleanup organizations or NGOs?

Not quite — we aren't organizing cleanups or trying to replace the amazing work that many NGOs and citizen groups already do. Instead, GEODHA helps **document the problem** and **guide people toward solutions** by making data visible and accessible. We also highlight active organizations so more people can discover and support them.

#### 🌍 What's the bigger goal?

We believe that **awareness + data = impact**. Waste issues don't go away just by cleaning up — they resurface if deeper causes (like infrastructure gaps or poor planning) are ignored. By collecting data and sharing it openly, we hope to help identify those root causes and push for systemic solutions — such as better waste processing centers, targeted segregation campaigns or even new landfills with better practices.
