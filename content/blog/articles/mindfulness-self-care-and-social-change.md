### *When the chaos feels too loud, the first step isn't outward. It's inward.*

Read this guide on how change begins with self, and simple practices to have more energy to tackle social issues.

---

#### Does trying to "change the world" feel… impossible?

For a lot of people, there may simply be too much going on in their lives to have the space to think about social or environmental causes. Other times it can feel like a lost cause trying to change people's poor habits. Seeing news of various crises can feel daunting and overwhelming — a relentless scroll through what's broken.

But is it all hopeless?

---

#### Social change requires a certain energy.

This is the energy that keeps you from burning out, that gives you the persistence to communicate and advocate for issues, and to act with clarity rather than frustration.

Mindfulness practices can help provide this energy.

---

#### The Two Minds

Before diving into practices, it helps to understand why mindfulness works at all.

We all carry two minds: a **higher mind** — governing intelligence, consciousness, and logical reasoning — and a **lower mind**, driven by senses and primal instinct. When the higher mind is weak, the lower mind takes over in a blind pursuit of sensory gratification, making us prone to stress, reactivity, and addiction.

Scientific research supports this. Studies show that regular meditation grows gray matter in the brain, the tissue associated with intelligence, reasoning, and emotional regulation *(Lazar et al., 2005)*. Strengthening the higher mind isn't just mysticism — it's neuroscience too.

---

#### One of the best practices is Meditation.

Meditation strengthens the higher mind. It provides clarity amidst the bustle and chaos of daily life, and reduces anxiety, fear, and other negative emotions over time. No other practice tames the mind more effectively.

That said — for many people, meditation can initially feel very difficult. The mind seems chaotic, restless, unable to sit still.

> *Meditation initially is like sweeping a dusty room. The dust flies up everywhere and seems chaotic — but with consistent effort, it eventually settles and clears.*

If sitting in silence feels like facing a void, try using sound (mantra chanting) or visualization to ease the process. There are many forms of meditation; the key is finding what works for you.

---

#### We also get energy from nature.

Spending time outdoors is one of the simplest and most underrated rechargers. This can mean getting some sunlight, visiting a clean lake for fresh air, or just walking barefoot on grass.

---

#### If all of this feels hard, begin with simpler practices.

You don't need to jump straight into deep meditation. Start where you are:

- Walking, running, sports, or yoga
- **Pranayama** — the yoga practice of breathing through alternate nostrils, which calms the nervous system and can provide immense energy
- A gratitude journal
- Drinking enough water
- Consistent sleep and good posture
- Prayer, if that resonates with you

> **If you already practice any of these — keep going. The key is consistency.**

---

#### A common trap: doom-scrolling.

A prevalent form of stress relief today — one affecting all generations — is doom-scrolling. It creates a vicious cycle: shortened attention spans, a constant sense of overwhelm, and temporary distraction without any real relief. Short-form video provides a quick escape but does nothing to address the underlying stress. Recognising this pattern is the first step to breaking it.

---

#### Recharged and empowered, you may just find the energy to begin.

Consider this example: for the problem of littering, cleanups are essential — but without education and awareness, the same habits repeat. Sustained changemaking requires the ability to communicate, to persist, to teach with patience rather than frustration.

Mindfulness gives you that. It's what allowed a simple household conversation about waste segregation to gradually shift into a family habit — not through force, but through consistent, calm persistence over time.
